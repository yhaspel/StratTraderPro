Setting your risk profile

Your risk profile is how StratTraderPro decides how big each trade should be — and when to stop trading to protect your account. Until you set one up, alerts trade exactly the quantity your strategy asks for. Once you save a profile, every alert is automatically sized to fit these limits before it reaches your broker.

You're always in control: nothing here places trades on its own. These settings only ever make a trade smaller or hold it back — they never make it bigger than your strategy asked for.

The fields, in plain terms

SettingWhat it does
Risk per trade (%) The most of your account you're willing to put at risk on a single trade — from just above 0% up to 5%. We combine this with your stop distance (below) to work out the position size. A smaller number means smaller, safer positions.
Max position (%) A ceiling on how much of your account any one position can take, up to 50%. Even if the risk math would buy more, we clamp the position to this share so you're never over-concentrated in one name.
Max concurrent The most positions you want open at the same time (at least 1).
Daily loss limit ($ and %) Your line in the sand for one day. If your losses for the day reach either the dollar amount or the percentage, the daily-loss circuit breaker trips: we flatten your positions and stop new trades for the rest of the day (it unlocks the next trading day). The percentage caps at 25%.
Leverage cap How much borrowing (leverage) you'll allow. Leave it at 1.0 to trade with your own capital only.
Permitted asset classes Which kinds of instruments you allow — Stocks, ETFs, Options, Futures. Must have at least one. Alerts for anything not on this list are turned away.
Soft stop (%) An early caution level. If your account is down this much for the day, new trades are automatically sized to half — you keep trading, just smaller, while things are rough.
Hard stop (%) Your deeper drawdown limit. It must be a larger number than your soft stop — soft stop trims you first, hard stop is the outer boundary.
Strict mode An extra guard for falling markets. With strict mode on, new buy (long) trades are turned away while the market regime is BEAR or CRISIS — you won't be sized into new longs into a downturn. Leave it off to size those trades down instead of blocking them.
ATR factor How wide to set the stop we size against, as a multiple of ATR (see below). A larger factor means a wider stop, which means smaller positions.

What is ATR?

ATR stands for Average True Range — a simple measure of how much a stock typically moves in a day, averaged over the last two weeks. A calm, steady stock has a small ATR; a jumpy, volatile one has a large ATR.

We use it to place a sensible stop: your ATR factor multiplied by the stock's ATR is roughly how far the price would have to move against you before the trade is stopped out. A wider stop (bigger ATR or bigger factor) means we buy fewer shares for the same risk, so a volatile stock automatically gets a smaller position than a calm one. If we don't have enough recent data to compute ATR for a symbol, we fall back to a conservative stop of 2% of the price.

How your settings shape each trade

When an alert comes in, we start from your risk per trade and the current market regime (calmer markets get your full risk, choppy or falling markets get a reduced share, and a full-blown crisis pauses new trades entirely). We size the position against your ATR-based stop, then apply your caps and adjustments in order:

  • the max position clamp keeps any one trade from getting too big;
  • a small sentiment tilt nudges long trades up slightly when news is strongly positive, or down when it's clearly negative;
  • the soft stop halves new trades if you're already down for the day.

Every decision is recorded — you can open any order and see exactly how its size was worked out, which inputs were used, and why. If a trade was turned away you'll see the reason (for example REGIME_CRISIS in a crisis, or REGIME_SIDE_MISMATCH for a new long in a downturn with strict mode on).

Changing your risk profile requires your account to have two-factor authentication enabled, and applies to new alerts from that point on — it doesn't resize positions you already hold.

Good to know

  • Invalid combinations are rejected as you save — for example a risk-per-trade above 5%, a max position above 50%, or a soft stop that isn't smaller than your hard stop.
  • The Defaults button resets every field to sensible starting values if you want to begin fresh.
  • Your daily-loss breaker and kill switches share the same controls — see Using the kill switch for how to halt trading yourself at any time.
