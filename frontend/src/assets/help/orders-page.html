Reading the Orders page

The Orders page is the record of every order your strategies placed — across all your connected brokers. Each row is one order; open it to see its full lifecycle and every fill. This is where you go to answer "did that alert actually trade, and how?"

Orders here come from your strategies firing (a TradingView alert into your webhook), so a new row appears within a few seconds of an alert. If you expect an order and don't see one, check the strategy's activity view for a rejection reason first.

The columns

ColumnMeaning
TimeWhen the order was created (your alert fired).
BrokerWhich broker it routed to — Alpaca or TradeStation.
StrategyThe strategy that placed it.
SymbolThe instrument (a stock/ETF ticker, or an option/future contract).
SideBuy or Sell (options also show open/close, e.g. Buy to open).
QtyQuantity ordered.
TypeOrder type — MKT, LMT, STP, or STP_LMT.
StatusWhere the order is in its life (see below).
FillsHow much has filled so far.
P&LProfit/loss for the resulting position, where available.

What the statuses mean

StatusMeaning
PENDING_SUBMITWe've accepted the order and are sending it to the broker.
SUBMITTEDThe broker has it and is working it (a limit/stop order waiting for its price sits here).
PARTIALPartially filled — some quantity has executed, the rest is still working.
FILLEDFully executed.
CANCELLEDCancelled before fully filling (by you, or expired).
REJECTEDThe broker (or a pre-trade check) refused it — the reason is on the detail drawer.
A market order that sits at SUBMITTED and doesn't fill is almost always because it was sent outside US market hours — market orders queue at the broker until the next open. This is expected, not a bug.

Filtering the list

Use the filters above the table to narrow it down. They combine, and the CSV export (below) respects whatever filters are active:

  • Date range — defaults to today; widen it to look back.
  • Broker — Alpaca or TradeStation.
  • Strategy — a single strategy.
  • Status — e.g. only REJECTED to hunt down problems, or only FILLED.

The table is paginated (25 orders per page); page through with the controls at the bottom. An order's link is shareable — opening it deep-links straight to that order's detail.

The detail drawer

Click any row to open its detail drawer, which shows the order's whole story:

  • Lifecycle events in order — submitted → acknowledged by the broker → partial fill(s) → filled / complete (or cancelled / rejected). This is the timeline of what happened and when.
  • Fills — each execution with its quantity and price, so a partially-filled order shows exactly how it filled.
  • Broker codes — the broker's own order id and any status / rejection code, useful if you need to cross-check in the broker's dashboard or contact support.
  • Raw payload (masked) — the underlying request/response for reference. Secrets and full account numbers are masked — the drawer never shows sensitive values.

Exporting to CSV

Click Export CSV to download the currently-filtered set of orders. The file uses ISO-8601 timestamps and includes, per order: created time, broker, strategy, symbol, asset class, side, quantity, filled quantity, order type, status, and any reject reason. Because it follows your active filters, narrow the list first (e.g. one strategy, this month) to export exactly what you want.

The export is capped at a large number of rows per download — if you're pulling a very long history, tighten the date range and export in chunks.

Good to know

  • Orders from all your brokers show in one list — use the Broker column/filter to tell Alpaca and TradeStation apart.
  • The Positions widget on your dashboard is the live holdings view; the Orders page is the historical record of how you got there.
  • If your dashboard positions ever look out of step with your broker, the app reconciles them automatically in the background toward the broker's numbers.
