Reading the tearsheet

The tearsheet is the report a completed backtest produces, in three formats — JSON (the canonical, hashable numbers), a self-contained HTML report, and a print-ready PDF. All of the metrics below are computed on the out-of-sample curve — the stitched-together test windows the strategy never trained on — so they reflect how the strategy did on unseen data, not on the data it was tuned against.

One report, per symbol. Every symbol in the run gets its own metrics block and its own curves. There's no blended portfolio number — each symbol stands on its own.

The metrics

MetricWhat it means
Total return The out-of-sample equity's overall gain or loss, as a fraction of the starting cash. +0.25 = up 25% across the whole test span.
CAGR Compound annual growth rate — total return expressed as a smooth per-year rate (annualized on 252 trading days). Lets you compare runs of different lengths.
Sharpe Return per unit of total volatility (mean daily return ÷ standard deviation, annualized). Higher is better; a Sharpe below ~1 is generally weak. Rewards smooth returns, penalizes choppiness in both directions.
Sortino Like Sharpe but only penalizes downside volatility. Usually higher than Sharpe; a big gap between the two means the wobble is mostly upside.
MAR CAGR ÷ the absolute maximum drawdown. A pain-adjusted return: "how much did I earn per unit of worst-case loss." Above 0.5 is decent; above 1 is strong.
Max drawdown The deepest peak-to-trough drop in equity, as a fraction. -0.30 means at its worst the account was down 30% from a prior high. This is the stomach test.
% win Share of closed trades that made money. High isn't automatically good — a strategy can win often and still lose overall if the losses are big (check profit factor and expectancy alongside it).
Profit factor Gross profit ÷ gross loss. Above 1 is profitable; 1.5+ is solid. Shown as blank when there were no losing trades to divide by.
Avg win / Avg loss The average dollar P&L of winning trades and of losing trades. The ratio between them is the reward-to-risk of a typical trade.
Expectancy Average P&L per trade across all trades (wins and losses together). The single "what do I make per trade on average" number. Negative expectancy = the strategy loses money per trade regardless of win rate.
Exposure % Fraction of bars the strategy was actually holding a position. Low exposure with a good return means the edge is concentrated; 100% means it's always in the market.
Turnover Total traded notional relative to average equity — how much the strategy churns. High turnover makes results more sensitive to the cost assumptions on the cover.
Trades Number of closed trades. Very few trades means the metrics above rest on a small sample — treat them with more caution.
PBO Probability of Backtest Overfitting — the overfitting risk score. See Interpreting PBO; above 0.5 gets a warning badge.
Sharpe stability
(mean / σ)		 The mean and standard deviation of Sharpe across the walk-forward windows. A high mean with a low σ means the edge showed up consistently window after window. A high mean with a large σ means one or two lucky windows carried it — far less trustworthy than a single headline Sharpe suggests.

The charts

  • Out-of-sample equity curve — the stitched test-window equity over time. Smooth and rising is the goal; a staircase with long flat or falling stretches tells you where the edge disappeared.
  • Drawdown — how far below the prior peak the curve was, over time. Depth and duration both matter.
  • Monthly returns heatmap — return by calendar month; makes seasonal patterns and bad stretches obvious.
  • Per-window Sharpe — one bar per walk-forward window. This is the picture behind Sharpe stability: you want a row of similar bars, not one giant bar and a lot of small ones.

The past-performance disclaimer

This disclaimer is always on the report — it cannot be turned off. Past performance is not indicative of future results. A tearsheet is a historical simulation on the strategy and cost assumptions shown, not actual trading. Simulated results have inherent limitations, don't capture every real-world friction (liquidity, borrow, halts, corporate actions), and can be over-fit to the sample period. Nothing here is investment advice. Do not trade capital you cannot afford to lose.

Read the tearsheet as evidence, not a promise. The most useful signals of robustness aren't the headline return — they're low PBO, stable per-window Sharpe, and a drawdown you could actually live through. Next: Interpreting PBO.
