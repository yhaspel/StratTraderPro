How StratTraderPro reads the news

StratTraderPro watches breaking financial news through the day, works out which stocks each story is about, and turns the tone of that coverage into a sentiment score — one number for the whole market, plus a read per stock. It's designed to answer "what's the mood of the tape right now, and on my names?" at a glance.

Sentiment is information, not an instruction. It doesn't place or block trades on its own in this release — it's context to help you read the day. Later releases use it as one input to position sizing.

How a story becomes a score

Every incoming article goes through the same four steps:

  1. Ingest. We pull headlines and filings from several news sources (market news, SEC 8-K filings, trading-halt notices and more) every few minutes, and drop exact duplicates so the same story counts once.
  2. Tag the tickers. We work out which stocks the story is about — from tickers and $CASHTAG mentions in the text and from company names ("Apple Inc" → AAPL) — so the story lands on the right names.
  3. Score the tone. A fast financial language model reads every article and rates it positive, negative or neutral. For the stories that matter most — an 8-K, a trading halt, a guidance change — or ones the fast model is unsure about, a second, deeper model takes a closer look and adds an impact rating and a short summary.
  4. Blend over time. Each stock's recent articles are combined into a running score that leans on the freshest news — a story from an hour ago counts far more than one from this morning, and news older than a day drops out of the window.

The market sentiment number

At the top of the dashboard, the market sentiment reading is a single number on a scale from −1 to +1, blended across the stocks in the news (weighted toward the larger companies):

ReadingWhat it means
near +1Coverage is broadly upbeat — positive stories outweigh negative ones.
near 0Mixed or quiet — good and bad news roughly balance, or there's little of note.
near −1Coverage is broadly negative — the flow is dominated by bad news.

A history line next to it shows how the reading has moved over the last several days, so you can tell whether today's mood is a fresh shift or part of a longer stretch.

Because the score leans on recent news, it can move quickly when a big story breaks and then ease back on its own as that story ages out of the window — even with no new headlines. That's the recency weighting working, not the number glitching.

The recent-news feed

Click a stock (or open the news feed) to see the recent articles behind its score — the most impactful stories first. Each item shows the headline, the source, whether we flagged it as material (an 8-K, a halt, a guidance change), its tone, and — for the deeper-scored stories — an impact rating and a one-line summary.

We show a summary and a link, never the full article. Out of respect for the source's copyright, the feed gives you our short summary and a link out to read the original at the publisher — we don't republish the article body.

The "degraded" state

You may occasionally see a note that sentiment is running in a degraded state. That means the deeper second model is temporarily switched off, so scoring is running on the fast first model alone.

This is a graceful fallback, not an outage. You still get a valid market and per-stock sentiment score and the recent-news feed — you're simply missing the extra impact detail the deeper model adds on the biggest stories, until it's back on. Nothing you do is affected.

Good to know

  • Scores cover English-language news for US-listed stocks in this release; social media and non-English coverage may come later.
  • It refreshes on its own through the trading day — you don't need to do anything.
  • It's a context signal. Treat it as one more input to your own judgement, not a buy/sell trigger.
