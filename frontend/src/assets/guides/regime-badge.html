Understanding the Regime badge

The Regime badge on your dashboard is a one-glance read on what kind of market you're in right now — from calm and rising to stressed and falling. It's a market-wide signal (based on the broad US market, not any single stock), and it updates automatically through the trading day.

The regime is information, not an instruction. It doesn't place or block trades on its own in this release — it's context to help you read your strategies' behavior. Later releases use it as an input to position sizing.

What the badges mean

BadgeWhat it's telling you
BULL Risk-on. Broad participation, firm momentum, calm volatility, tight credit spreads — a healthy, rising market.
NEUTRAL Mixed. No clear risk-on or risk-off tilt — the signals roughly cancel out.
CHOP Choppy / sideways. The model sees a churning, directionless regime rather than a clean trend — common in range-bound markets.
BEAR Risk-off. Weak breadth, negative momentum, rising volatility or widening credit spreads — a falling or defensive market.
CRISIS Acute stress. Volatility and credit spreads are extreme — the most defensive reading.

How the regime is worked out

The badge blends two independent views of the market and combines them into one label:

  • A rule-based score. A transparent, always-on score built from market breadth (how many stocks are participating), volatility (VIX and related stress gauges), and credit spreads (how nervous the bond market is), plus momentum and a couple of macro signals. Each ingredient pushes the score toward risk-on or risk-off, landing in one of four bands from risk-on down to panic.
  • A statistical model (an HMM). A model trained on years of daily market data that learns recurring market "states" directly from the data — patterns the fixed rule can't see on its own. It's retrained automatically overnight and only replaces the previous model if it's at least as good.

The final badge is the combination of the two: the model can sharpen or override the rule's read when it's confident, while the rule keeps the whole thing explainable and always available.

The top-features popover

Hover (or tap) the badge to see why the market is being read this way. The popover lists the top contributing features — the signals moving the score the most right now — each with a z-score: how unusual that signal is versus its own recent history.

  • A positive z-score means the signal is running high versus its recent norm; negative means it's running low.
  • Bigger numbers (further from zero) mean more unusual — a +2.1 on breadth or a +2.5 on credit spreads is a strong contributor; a value near 0 is business as usual.
So a BEAR badge whose popover shows credit spreads and volatility both well above normal is telling you the read is driven by stress, not just weak prices — that's the reason code, in plain sight.

The "Rule-based only" state

If you see a "Rule-based only" note on the badge, the statistical model is temporarily unavailable — usually because its overnight retrain hasn't produced a fresh model in over 48 hours. When that happens the badge falls back to the transparent rule-based score alone.

This is a graceful fallback, not an outage. The badge still shows a valid regime and its top features — you're simply seeing the rule's read without the model's input until the model refreshes. Nothing you do is affected; it clears automatically once the next model trains successfully.

The history chart

Below the badge, the regime history chart shows how the market's regime has moved over recent months, so you can see whether today's read is a fresh shift or part of a longer stretch. Hover any point to see the reading for that day.

Good to know

  • The regime is market-wide — one read for the whole US market, not per-symbol. Per-symbol regimes may come in a later release.
  • It refreshes on its own during market hours; you don't need to do anything.
  • It's a context signal. Treat it as one more input to your own judgement, not a buy/sell trigger.
